# Secondhand sale listings

## When to Use

Use when asked for an asking price, a secondhand sales ad, or bilingual listing copy based on photos, packaging, or a named retailer's current price. This is a one-off pricing and copywriting task, not a recurring price watch.

## Procedure

1. Inventory the user's evidence first. For a ZIP of photos, list members and safely extract image files into the scratch directory; inspect every materially different photo with vision. Separate visible facts, the user's stated use/defects, and unverified assumptions. Do not infer the exact revision from a similar-looking current product: printed dimensions and logos may match across generations while the model name does not.
2. Identify the product as precisely as the evidence allows. Read packaging, model labels, dimensions, and photographed mechanisms; if a revision cannot be confirmed, use the generic model in the headline and explicitly call the retailer's current revision a comparison rather than the identical item. Don't assert an accessory is physically included solely because an instruction diagram depicts it; inspect photos or qualify the statement.
3. Fetch the live price from the retailer the user names. Check the exact currency, variant, availability and displayed price on the product page; cite the direct URL. When the listing is a different revision, say so. Search local comparable secondhand listings if possible; if none are verified, label the suggested price as a judgment against the retail anchor, not as a market statistic. Account for damage, replacement effort, age, and local collection/shipping. Offer an asking price and a realistic negotiation range, clearly distinct.
4. Draft the requested languages as ready-to-paste listings with matching factual details: title, price, practical use, honest reason for selling, usage duration, defects and workaround/repair, dimensions only if evidenced, and pickup location only as specific as the user permits. Be direct and natural, not a feature dump. Do not claim a broken fastener has been replaced, or that all other parts are flawless, without evidence. If requested, find and verify the official manufacturer's product-description URL; prefer that over a retailer link and do not silently substitute a different revision.
5. Prepare listing photos when requested: inspect each source image, crop excess surroundings without cutting off the product or important labels, and visually check the resulting files. Preserve originals. Use a whole-product shot as the lead image and a label/detail shot second. Attach the edited images to the deliverable itself, rather than merely saying they exist or sending unedited originals.
6. Keep citations and pricing rationale outside the paste-ready listing copy unless the user wants retail comparisons in the ad. When asked for separate Telegram-forwardable listings, deliver each language as a self-contained message with only the advertisement, requested product link, and its photos; keep status notes such as email delivery in a different message, because forwarding otherwise includes that chatter. If sending photos by email too, use the final edited versions, confirm an unspecified recipient before sending, and read back the sent message rather than treating the send result alone as proof. Do not publish the ad without explicit instruction and confirmation.
